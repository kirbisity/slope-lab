    How Slope Lab works

    1 · Build with maths

    Every track is the graph of y = f(x) between two x values. Open y = f(x), type an equation and add it. Use + − * / ^ ( ), sin cos tan sqrt abs log exp, pi and e; 2x and 3(x+1) work too.

    Or pick Sketch and draw with a finger or mouse; the line is smoothed so small wobbles do not become jumps. Erase removes a track, Start moves the flag.

    2 · Ride it

    Press Ride (Enter). The skier is on their own, but you can help:

    • ↓ / S hold to tuck: less drag, more speed
    • ← / A hold to brake: a hockey stop. Too fast or too steep and you catch an edge; on very steep ground it only slows you
    • Space / ↑ to pop off the snow
    • → / D spin, F backflip: tap once, even before a jump; it starts in the air and carries to the landing
    • R back to editing, P pause, M sound

    On a touchscreen the same three buttons appear at the bottom while you ride. Finish a slope and a ghost of your best run races you next time.

    3 · The physics

    On snow, gravity pulls along the slope (g·sin θ), friction pushes back (μ·N, μ = 0.045) and air drag grows with speed squared.

    Over a crest you leave the snow when v²κ > g·cos θ, where κ is the curvature. In the air you follow a parabola. When you land, only the speed into the slope hurts: over 4 m/s is a hard landing, over 11 m/s a wipeout. The dashed arc shows where you will land: green is soft, amber is hard, red is a crash.

    4 · Scoring

    Joy grows with speed above walking pace, faster in the air, +30 for every half turn and +60 for every backflip landed. Ouch grows with every hard landing or sharp kink. Score = 50 × Joy ÷ (Ouch + 1), so smooth lines beat rough ones.

    Start with Joyride, a new random slope each time with no maths needed. Each slope has three stars. Some need a clean run, others need you to go faster or fly longer. Challenges limit how many metres of track (ink) you can add.
